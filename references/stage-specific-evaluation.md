## Stage 1: OPPORTUNITY (Getting the Meeting)

**What Evan should be doing:**
- Outbound outreach via email, LinkedIn, and phone
- Personalized messages that reference the prospect's specific situation (trigger events, tech stack, recent funding, etc.)
- Multi-channel cadence: not just email — LinkedIn + email + phone
- Quick response to inbound leads (within 2 hours ideally)

**Email evaluation at this stage:**
- [ ] Is the outreach personalized to THIS company, or is it a template?
- [ ] Does it mention a specific trigger event or reason for reaching out?
- [ ] Is it concise? (Under 150 words — nobody reads long cold emails)
- [ ] Clear CTA: is he asking for a specific meeting time, or vaguely saying "let's chat"?
- [ ] Follow-up cadence: 3-5 touches over 2 weeks, then re-engage after 30 days?
- [ ] Multi-channel: is he pairing email with LinkedIn connection requests?

**Meeting evaluation at this stage:**
- N/A — no meetings yet at this stage. If Evan has a call, it's an intro/qualifying call.
- If there IS a quick intro call: is Evan qualifying (right ICP, has R&D, Canadian payroll) or just pitching?

**Red flags:**
- Sending generic templates to everyone
- Only using one channel (email only, no LinkedIn/phone)
- Giving up after 1-2 touches
- Not responding to inbound leads within 4 hours
- Prospect doesn't match SRED.ca ICP (reference `sred-brand-icp` skill)

## Stage 2: SR&ED ASSESSMENT (The Pitch Meeting)

This is Evan's main stage. He leads the call, walks through the deck, explains SR&ED, presents pricing, and qualifies the prospect.

**Meeting evaluation at this stage (full Barrows framework):**

### Before the meeting:
- [ ] Did Evan research the prospect? (Company website, LinkedIn, tech stack, previous SR&ED claims)
- [ ] Did he send a pre-meeting confirmation or agenda?
- [ ] Did he check if the right person will be on the call? (Decision-maker or technical lead)

### During the meeting:
- [ ] **Discovery before pitch:** Did Evan ask about the prospect's business BEFORE jumping into the deck? (What do you do? What does your R&D look like? Have you heard of SR&ED?)
- [ ] **Talk ratio:** Is he in the 50-60% range for a first pitch, or is he monologuing at 70%+?
- [ ] **Qualification questions:** Did he ask about Canadian employees/payroll, fiscal year-end, existing SR&ED claims, other government grants?
- [ ] **Objection handling:** When the prospect pushes back (cost, risk, "we tried this before"), does Evan address it directly or dodge it?
- [ ] **Urgency creation:** Does he explain the 18-month filing deadline? Help them understand the cost of waiting?
- [ ] **Guarantee explanation:** Did he clearly explain the 75% guarantee? (This is SRED.ca's differentiator — it should come up every time)
- [ ] **Pricing clarity:** Was the flat-fee model clearly explained and compared to contingency competitors?
- [ ] **Next steps — book the Tech Discovery:** Did Evan clearly set the next action as booking the Technical Discovery meeting with Logan? At this stage, the goal is to move the prospect to Part B of the two-part sale (Tech Discovery), NOT to ask for payment. Payment comes after Tech Discovery under the current process (changed 2026-04-20).
- [ ] **Small talk management:** How long before business starts? (>5 min of hockey talk = flag)

### After the meeting (same-day or next-morning follow-up):
- [ ] **Timeliness:** Follow-up sent within 4 hours of meeting end
- [ ] **Personal touch:** References something specific from the conversation
- [ ] **Value recap:** Restates what SR&ED could mean for THIS specific prospect
- [ ] **Tech Discovery booking link included:** The follow-up email should include Logan's calendar booking link for the Tech Discovery. (Payment link is NOT sent at this stage under the current process — it comes after Tech Discovery.)
- [ ] **Resources attached:** Presentation deck, interview prep guide, example project
- [ ] **Referral follow-through:** If Evan promised an intro (e.g., to Jane for IRAP, to another service), did he include it?
- [ ] **Guarantee mention:** Includes or links to the SR&ED guarantee
- [ ] **Pricing recap:** Restates the fee structure discussed
- [ ] **Info request:** Asks for company name, fiscal year-end, business number, web address
- [ ] **Professional signature:** Proper formatting, booking link, contact info

> **PROCESS NOTE:** The two-part sale is (A) Sales Pitch and (B) Technical Discovery. Payment is requested AFTER Part B, not after Part A. This rule changed on 2026-04-20 — see project history below.

**Known benchmark — the Sheldrake follow-up (Jan 28, 2026):**
Evan's follow-up to Scott Sheldrake / Numbered GameCo hits most items: coffee invite (personal touch), Jane Ramachandran referral included, interview prep guide attached, guarantee explained with link, pricing stated ($10K startup plan), five specific data points requested, engagement letter referenced, and "you have my phone number" as a personal close. (Note: that Jan 2026 email included a payment link under the OLD pre-April-20 process — under the current process, post-pitch follow-ups should include the Tech Discovery booking link instead, with payment coming later.)

## Stage 2 → Stage 3: BOOKING THE TECH DISCOVERY

The transition from Sales Pitch to Technical Discovery is the most important moment in the pipeline. Under the current process (since 2026-04-20), the goal here is **NOT to collect payment** — it's to qualify the prospect and book them with Logan for a deeper technical conversation.

**What needs to happen:**
1. Same-day follow-up email from Evan with the Tech Discovery booking link (Logan's calendar)
2. Prep guide + presentation + relevant resources attached
3. Prospect books the Tech Discovery
4. Evan briefs Logan before the meeting (context, concerns, fiscal year-end, etc.)

**Coach evaluation at this transition:**
- [ ] **Speed to booking link:** Same-day follow-up with Logan's calendar link? (Target: <4 hrs)
- [ ] **Internal handoff:** Did Evan brief Logan before the Tech Discovery on what was discussed in the pitch, prospect concerns, and any flags?
- [ ] **Qualification clarity:** If the prospect raised disqualifying issues during the pitch (no T4s, US-based dev team, no Canadian R&D), is Evan moving them to Tech Discovery anyway, or qualifying them out?
- [ ] **Follow-up cadence:** If the prospect hasn't booked within 3 business days, is Evan re-engaging?

**Red flags:**
- Asking for payment in the post-pitch follow-up email (this is the OLD process — retired April 2026)
- Booking link not sent in the post-pitch follow-up email
- No internal brief to Logan before Tech Discovery
- Disqualifying info raised in pitch (e.g., no T4 salaries) but deal moved to Tech Discovery anyway with no plan to address the gap

## PROCESS HISTORY: Why Payment Now Comes After Tech Discovery

**Before April 2026 (the OLD rule):** Payment was the gate between Sales Pitch and Tech Discovery. Prospects had to pay BEFORE Logan would meet with them.

**April 20, 2026 (the change):** During Evan's Session 2 coaching call, John surfaced friction in the payment-first flow — Evan was uncomfortable asking for payment immediately after the pitch because the prospect's eligibility wasn't yet established. Evan's exact words: *"I feel uncomfortable asking for it. I think it's way too soon. We have not even gotten anywhere above their actual eligibility... I'm not disagreeing conceptually, I'm just saying in practice... I think we should talk it through with Jude."* Evan took it to Jude immediately after the coaching call. Together they redesigned the process: payment now comes AFTER the Tech Discovery, before the deeper Technical Interview.

**Why this matters for coaching:** This is a Self-Determination Theory case study and John should treat it that way. The change wasn't top-down. It was: friction surfaced in coaching → Evan chose to use his voice rather than comply → Evan proposed the fix → company adopted it. This is Decision Ladder Rung 4-5 (he proposed, the company informed) and Motivation Ladder Level 4 (value alignment — he authored the change). Coach should reinforce this anytime a similar friction shows up in future sessions: *"What's your read? What would you change?"* — Evan's instinct to author beats compliance every time.

## Stage 3: TECHNICAL DISCOVERY (Logan/James Lead)

**Prerequisite:** Pitch (Stage 2) is complete and the prospect has agreed to a deeper technical conversation. Under the current process (changed 2026-04-20), **payment is requested AFTER this meeting, before the deeper Technical Interview / engagement starts.** That means the prospect has NOT yet paid when this meeting happens — so the trust transfer to Logan/James is critical. The prospect is deciding whether to sign up with SRED.ca based partly on how this meeting goes.

Logan or James runs the technical content. But Evan has a defined, non-trivial job here.

### Evan's #1 Job at Tech Discovery: Transfer Trust to Logan

Because the prospect hasn't paid yet, the most important thing Evan does at Tech Discovery is **make the prospect feel safe and in good hands with Logan.** A two-sentence Logan intro doesn't do this. The intro needs to land.

**The Logan intro template — Evan should be able to deliver this from memory:**

> "I'll intro Logan. Logan is our original founder. He's been doing SR&ED for about 20 years. He's a CPA, came up through KPMG with a tax specialization. The first SR&ED claim he ever did was at a dental software company right out of university — they asked him to take it over. Later, while he was a manager at KPMG, he was personally recruited by Alex Popa, the founder of Boast AI, and was one of the first five employees there. Safe to say, with Logan overseeing every aspect of your SR&ED claim, you are in good hands."

**Why each piece matters:**
- "Original founder" — signals continuity, ownership, pride in the work
- "20 years" — depth of experience, not a new firm
- "CPA from KPMG with tax specialization" — institutional credibility, not a shred-only consultant
- "Dental software company / asked to take over the claim" — humanizes him, shows he's been doing this since the start of his career
- "Personally recruited by Alex Popa / first 5 at Boast" — competitive signal, especially when the prospect knows or has used Boast (most do)
- "You are in good hands" — the explicit safety / trust frame

**Coaching evaluation — trust transfer check:**
- [ ] **Logan intro depth:** Did Evan deliver the full intro, or a 1-2 sentence summary? (Thin intro = trust-transfer failure)
- [ ] **Tailoring to context:** If the prospect named a competitor in the pitch (e.g., Boast, contingency-fee firm), did Evan lean into the relevant part of Logan's bio? (Especially: if Boast was mentioned, the Alex Popa recruitment story is GOLD)
- [ ] **Confidence:** Does Evan deliver it like he believes it, or like he's checking a box?
- [ ] **The close phrase:** "You are in good hands" (or equivalent) — did he land it?

### Other Tech Discovery Evaluation

- [ ] **Pre-meeting prep:** Did Evan send the prospect the interview prep guide and set expectations? Did he confirm the right people from the prospect's side will attend (technical lead/CTO)?
- [ ] **Contract/pricing recap:** If the prospect asks about pricing or terms, Evan owns the answer — not Logan
- [ ] **Handoff quality:** Smooth transition Evan → Logan, no awkward "uh, I'll pass it"
- [ ] **Engagement during Logan's portion:** Evan should be MOSTLY SILENT once Logan takes the technical lead. This is Logan's meeting. Evan's job during the technical portion is to actively listen (take notes, capture action items, watch for closing signals) — NOT to interject, add color, or share his read on technical points. Disappearing during Logan's flow is the correct behavior, not a coaching gap. The exception: if the prospect asks about pricing/terms or contract — Evan owns that immediately, even mid-flow.
- [ ] **Payment ask at close:** Under the new process, Evan owns the payment-link ask at meeting end — clean, confident, with a clear next step (Technical Interview booking after payment)
- [ ] **Post-meeting follow-up:** Same-day email with Stripe link, prep docs, and clear scheduling for the Technical Interview

**Email evaluation at this stage:**
- [ ] **Pre-meeting prep:** Did Evan send the prospect the interview prep guide and set expectations for the technical meeting? Did he confirm who from the prospect's side should attend (technical lead/CTO)?
- [ ] **Post-meeting follow-up:** Same-day with Stripe payment link, prep docs, Technical Interview booking link
- [ ] **Internal handoff:** Did Evan brief Logan/James before the call? (Context about the prospect, what was discussed in the pitch, any concerns raised, fiscal year-end, company details)

**Red flags:**
- Thin Logan intro (1-2 sentences) — failed trust transfer
- Boast or other competitor named in the pitch and Evan didn't lean into Logan's competitive bio
- Evan not on the call at all (he should at least open and close)
- Prospect asks about pricing/terms and Logan has to answer
- No follow-up email after the technical meeting
- Forgetting the Stripe payment link in the follow-up (under the new process, this is the gate to engagement)

## Stage 4: FOLLOW-UP (Post-Discovery, Active Client)

Technical Discovery is complete. The claim work is underway or being planned. Evan's role shifts to client success and ensuring nothing falls through the cracks.

**Email evaluation at this stage:**
- [ ] **Onboarding confirmation:** Has Evan confirmed the quarterly meeting schedule with the client?
- [ ] **Billing running smoothly:** Are monthly payments coming through without issues? (Reference the Sky High ERP Stripe invoice issue as an example of what can go wrong)
- [ ] **Follow-up cadence:** If no response within 3 business days, is Evan following up?
- [ ] **Objection handling in writing:** If the prospect has concerns (pricing, timing, risk), is Evan addressing them clearly in email?
- [ ] **Urgency reinforcement:** Is he reminding them of the filing deadline or fiscal year-end timing?
- [ ] **Multi-channel:** If email isn't getting responses, is Evan picking up the phone?
- [ ] **Escalation awareness:** If a deal stalls here for >7 days, is Evan flagging it to Jude?

**Red flags:**
- Contract sent but no follow-up for >5 days
- Prospect went quiet and Evan stopped reaching out
- Multiple follow-ups that are just "checking in" with no value-add
- Deal sitting in this stage >30 days without movement

## Stage 5: CLOSED / WON (Onboarding)

Client signed. Now it's about smooth onboarding and transition to the delivery team.

**Email evaluation at this stage:**
- [ ] **Welcome/thank you:** Did Evan send a congratulatory email welcoming them as a client?
- [ ] **Onboarding checklist:** Did he clearly communicate next steps (first quarterly meeting, what to prepare, who their project manager is)?
- [ ] **Payment confirmation:** Is the Stripe billing set up correctly and confirmed?
- [ ] **Internal handoff:** Did Evan brief Logan/James with everything from the sales process? (Company background, prospect concerns, key contacts, fiscal year-end, etc.)
- [ ] **Responsiveness:** Is Evan still responsive to client questions, or does he go dark after the close?

**Red flags:**
- Client has to chase Evan for onboarding information
- Logan/James have no context about the client when they start
- Billing/admin issues (like the Sky High ERP Stripe invoice issue) not resolved promptly
- Client CC's Jude because Evan isn't responding

## Stage 6: CLOSED / LOST (Post-Mortem)

**Coaching opportunity — every loss is a lesson:**
- Why did it die? (No SR&ED fit? Price objection? Went with a competitor? Just went dark?)
- At what stage did it stall? (The earlier it dies, the more likely it's a qualification issue. Later = closing issue.)
- Was there a pattern? (Third deal lost because Evan didn't follow up? That's a trend.)
- Did Evan ask for feedback from the prospect? (Barrows: "You learn more from the deals you lose")

## How the Coach Uses This

Before evaluating ANY meeting or email thread, the coach MUST:

1. **Check the deal stage in HubSpot** for that prospect
2. **Apply the correct evaluation criteria** for that stage
3. **Note where Evan is strong vs. weak at each stage** — patterns across deals at the same stage are more valuable than one-off observations

For example:
- If Evan is great at Stage 2 (pitch) but deals keep dying at Stage 4 (follow-up), the coaching focus should be on closing discipline, not pitch technique.
- If deals at Stage 1 (opportunity) aren't converting to Stage 2 (assessment), the focus should be on outreach quality and cadence.
- If Stage 3 (technical discovery) meetings go well but the contract isn't sent promptly, the focus should be on administrative follow-through.
